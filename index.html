      What you're seeing

        Light at a walking pace

        In Slowlight, light crawls along at about the speed you can run, so the strange effects of Einstein's relativity happen right in front of you.

        The big ideas

        • Light takes time to arrive. You see everything as it was when its light set off, so far-off things are seen later, and fast things where they used to be.
        • Moving light changes colour. Coming toward you it shifts toward blue, going away toward red, and past the ends of the rainbow it turns invisible.
        • Speed bends your view. The faster you go, the more the world crowds into the way ahead.
        • Fast clocks run slow, and fast things get shorter. Board a train or a starship and come back younger than everyone who stayed.

        In the real world

        Real light covers 1.08 billion km an hour, so all of this happens around us too, just too small or too fast to notice.

        • The sunlight on your face left the Sun about eight minutes ago, and the Moon you see is the Moon of a second and a quarter ago.
        • The clocks on GPS satellites have to be corrected for relativity. Without that, your phone's position would drift by about 10 km a day.
        • Muons made by cosmic rays high in the sky live only two millionths of a second, yet plenty reach the ground, because their clocks run slow.
        • In 1971, atomic clocks flown around the world on airliners came back out of step with ones that stayed home, by as much as relativity predicts.

        Reading the screen

        You is your own watch; World is the clock of the place around you. The gap between them is how much younger you are. In the observation log, seen is when an event's light reached you, and happened is when it took place in your frame, with the light's travel time taken out.
